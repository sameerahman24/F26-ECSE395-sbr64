##Reflection

The guest lecture on engineering standards made it very clear why conventions occur, which is the fact that the decision that works locally fails when scaling occurs. What I found interesting and memorable from the lecture is the example of the Mars Climate Orbiter, where one of the teams used imperial units and another used metric units, there was no notice of it, and consequently the $327.6 million space probe was lost. This really makes me realize how neglecting conventions doesn't reduce overheads but shifts them further into future and makes it much more expensive. Another point which seemed quite important was the fact that blind adherence to convention doesn't help either; the engineer must make up his mind about whether the chosen convention fits or not. It seemed to be relevant to our own design process right now, where the needfinding/needs/requirements structure is a standard we're using, but it doesn't replace actually understanding Ms. Cina's situation.
